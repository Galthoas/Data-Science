# Certification Paths — Costs and How to Get Them Free (or Cheap)

Every course in this roadmap is **free to learn from**. Some certificates cost money if you want the verified credential to put on a resume or LinkedIn. This page lays out, honestly, what each costs and how to reduce that cost.

## Coursera certificates (Google Data Analytics, Google Advanced Data Analytics, IBM Data Science)

All three are Coursera "Professional Certificates," which means:

- **Auditing the course content is free**, but audit mode on Coursera typically doesn't give you graded assignments or the certificate.
- **Full paid access is a monthly subscription** (Coursera Plus or per-certificate pricing, currently in roughly the $40–50/month range depending on current promotions — check the live price on the course page, as this changes).
- **Financial aid is available on Coursera** for many courses and specializations. Look for a "Financial aid available" link under the enroll button — you fill out a short form and are typically approved within a couple of days. This is the most reliable route to earning these certificates for free.
- **A practical cost-minimizing strategy:** since the roadmap in this repo has you doing the actual learning first via the free MIT/Harvard/Kaggle/freeCodeCamp material, you can move through the *paid* Coursera track quickly once you subscribe — much of the content will be review — potentially finishing within a single month's subscription instead of the 3–6 months Google/IBM suggest for someone starting from zero. One month's subscription for three certificates, timed to overlap when you're ready for all of them, is a realistic low-cost approach if financial aid isn't approved.

## HarvardX Professional Certificate in Data Science (edX)

- **Auditing every one of the 9 courses is completely free**, no application, no time limit in most cases — you get 100% of the learning content.
- A **verified certificate** for each individual course currently costs roughly $50 (edX pricing varies by course and changes over time — check the live price on each course page), and the full 9-course Professional Certificate credential requires verified certificates in all 9.
- **edX offers financial assistance** for many courses — look for "Financial Assistance" on the course enrollment page.
- Because the skills matter more than the credential for your stated goal, a reasonable approach: audit all 9 for free, and only pay for verified certificates on the 2–3 courses most relevant to the jobs you're targeting (commonly Probability, Inference and Modeling, and Machine Learning) if budget is tight.

## CS50P (Harvard, via Harvard OCW — NOT via edx.org)

**Note:** CS50x (the general intro-to-CS course, which opens with a week of Scratch block-programming before reaching real code) was dropped from this roadmap's Phase 1 in favor of Python for Everybody (Py4E) and, optionally, MIT 6.0001 — see [`phases/01-programming-foundations/README.md`](phases/01-programming-foundations/README.md) for why. CS50P (the Python-only course, no blocks) is still part of the plan, and everything below still applies to it.

This one only works if you enroll on the right site. Two platforms offer the exact same CS50 content, and only one of them ever gives you a free certificate:

- **edx.org / learning.edx.org (wrong site for a free certificate):** edX only offers a paid "Verified" certificate here, currently **$219**. This is true whether you pick "Audit" or "Verified" during enrollment — edX simply does not issue a free certificate on its own platform, for CS50 or any course. If you enroll through edx.org, the only certificate available to you is the $219 one, full stop.
- **cs50.harvard.edu/python/ (Harvard OCW — the right site):** identical lectures, problem sets, and final project, but accessed and submitted through Harvard's own site instead of edX's course pages. Score 70%+ on all problem sets and the final project, and a free certificate (no ID verification, but real graded work) unlocks automatically — genuinely $0, no email, no ambiguity. You'll be asked to create a free edX account along the way, but only as a login used to submit and track assignments — you never go through edx.org's enrollment or track-select flow, and never see the $219 offer.
- **Practical instruction: start at https://cs50.harvard.edu/python/ directly (not edx.org, not a Google search result that lands you on an edx.org listing) and work week by week using the submission instructions on that site.**
- Source: [Class Central, "Harvard CS50 in 2026: How to Get a Free Certificate"](https://www.classcentral.com/report/cs50-free-certificate/) (updated March 2026) — confirms the $219 edX price and the OCW-only free-certificate path directly.

## MIT OpenCourseWare (18.06 Linear Algebra, 18.05 Probability & Statistics, 6.0002)

- **Completely free, no certificate offered at all** — OCW is published course material, not a credentialed platform. There is nothing to pay here ever. The value is 100% the learning, which is exactly what Phase 2 and Phase 5 use it for.

## Kaggle Learn

- **Completely free**, including the completion certificates/badges. No paid tier exists for Kaggle Learn.

## freeCodeCamp

- **Completely free**, including all certifications. freeCodeCamp is a nonprofit and has never charged for certificates.

## fast.ai / Stanford CS229 / DeepLearning.AI Deep Learning Specialization

- **fast.ai and CS229 lecture videos: completely free**, no certificate offered.
- **Deep Learning Specialization (Coursera): same audit/financial-aid situation as the Google/IBM certificates above.**

## Bottom line budget

If you pursue every certificate at full price with no financial aid, budget roughly **$150–300 total** (one to a few months of Coursera access, plus a handful of edX verified certificates) — a small fraction of a traditional bootcamp or degree. If financial aid is approved where offered, this can come close to **$0**. Either way, it's worth doing the free learning first (as this roadmap is structured) and deciding on paid credentials only once you know which ones your target employers actually ask for.
