# Master Roadmap

Pace assumption: **10–15 hrs/week**. Estimates below use that pace; adjust freely — the tracker matters more than the calendar.

| # | Phase | Duration | Hours | Core outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | [Setup](phases/00-setup/README.md) | 1–2 wks | ~15 hrs | Python, Git, GitHub, editor all working; this repo initialized |
| 1 | [Programming Foundations](phases/01-programming-foundations/README.md) | 8–10 wks | ~110 hrs | Solid Python; working SQL; comfortable in the command line |
| 2 | [Math & Statistics Foundations](phases/02-math-stats-foundations/README.md) | 10–12 wks | ~140 hrs | Linear algebra, probability, and statistics at the level ML actually uses |
| 3 | [Data Analysis & Visualization](phases/03-data-analysis-visualization/README.md) | 6–8 wks | ~90 hrs | Real EDA workflow: pandas, cleaning, visualization, a finished analysis |
| 4 | [Harvard Data Science Certificate](phases/04-harvard-data-science-certificate/README.md) | 10–14 wks | ~150 hrs | HarvardX's 9-course Professional Certificate in Data Science, completed |
| 5 | [Machine Learning](phases/05-machine-learning/README.md) | 10–12 wks | ~140 hrs | Supervised/unsupervised ML, model evaluation, a deployed-quality model |
| 6 | [Deep Learning & Advanced Topics](phases/06-deep-learning-advanced/README.md) | 6–8 wks | ~90 hrs | Neural nets, one deep learning project (vision or NLP) |
| 7 | [Portfolio Capstone Projects](phases/07-portfolio-projects/README.md) | 6–8 wks | ~90 hrs | 3–4 polished, documented, GitHub-hosted projects |
| 8 | [Certifications & Job Prep](phases/08-certifications-job-prep/README.md) | 4–6 wks | ~70 hrs | Certificates earned, resume/LinkedIn ready, interview-practiced |

**Total: ~52–70 weeks (≈ 12–16 months) at 10–15 hrs/week, ~895 hours.**

Phases 3 and onward all feed projects into `projects/` — you are building your portfolio the entire time, not just at the end.

## Sequencing notes

- **Phases 1 and 2 can run in parallel** if you prefer variety week to week (e.g., programming on weeknights, math on weekends). They don't depend on each other, only both need to be reasonably solid before Phase 4.
- **Phase 4 (HarvardX) overlaps in content with Phases 2–3.** That's intentional — repetition at increasing depth is how the material actually sticks, and HarvardX moves faster than a first pass would. If you've already internalized a topic, skim that HarvardX module instead of doing it start to finish.
- **Phase 6 (Deep Learning) is the one phase you can compress or skip** if your target roles are analytics/ML-generalist rather than deep-learning-specialist. Note that choice in your `STUDY-LOG-TEMPLATE.md` so future-you remembers why.
- **Certificates (Phase 8) can be started earlier.** The Google and IBM Professional Certificates are structured so you could technically enroll and work through them in parallel with Phases 1–5 rather than waiting — see [`CERTIFICATION-PATHS.md`](CERTIFICATION-PATHS.md) for the trade-offs either way.

## What "doctorate-equivalent" means in this plan, honestly

A PhD is original research plus a dissertation defended before a committee — no self-study plan, free or paid, replicates that credential. What this roadmap targets instead, and can honestly deliver, is the working knowledge a PhD-trained data scientist uses on the job: the same linear algebra (MIT 18.06), the same probability and inference (MIT 18.05, HarvardX), the same modeling methods (Stanford/DeepLearning.AI's Machine Learning Specialization), applied to real data through real projects. That's the bar this plan is built to hit, and it's a bar that gets people hired.

If, after finishing this roadmap, you want to go further toward the research side, the natural next step is the [MIT MicroMasters in Statistics and Data Science](https://micromasters.mit.edu/ds/) (edX, audit free, rigorous, credit-bearing if you later apply to a master's) — noted here as an optional Phase 9 for later, not included in the hour estimates above.
