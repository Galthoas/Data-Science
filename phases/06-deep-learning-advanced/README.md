# Phase 6 — Deep Learning & Advanced Topics (6–8 weeks, ~90 hrs)

Deep learning isn't required for every Data Scientist role, but having genuine, hands-on exposure to it — not just "I've heard of neural networks" — is what separates a candidate who can grow into ML Engineer / applied AI work from one who can't. Pick **one** of the two paths below; both are excellent and free, and they teach the same material from opposite directions.

## Option A: Deep Learning Specialization — DeepLearning.AI (Andrew Ng)
**~70–80 hrs**, auditable free on Coursera.
- https://www.coursera.org/specializations/deep-learning
- Top-down-then-bottom: starts from neural network fundamentals and builds up through CNNs, sequence models, and transformers, with heavy emphasis on the underlying math (backpropagation derived from first principles, which connects directly back to the linear algebra and calculus from Phase 2).
- Best choice if you want to deeply understand *why* deep learning works, not just how to use it.

## Option B: Practical Deep Learning for Coders — fast.ai
**~60–70 hrs.**
- https://course.fast.ai/
- Bottom-up-then-top: gets you training real, working, state-of-the-art models in the first lesson, and unpacks the theory progressively as you go. Built by Jeremy Howard specifically to be accessible to people without a heavy math background — a good fit if Phase 2's math still feels shaky and you want a confidence boost through practical wins first.
- The accompanying free book: https://fastai.github.io/fastbook2e/

## Which to choose

If Phase 2 (math foundations) felt solid and you enjoy understanding mechanisms, choose **Option A**. If you'd rather build working things first and backfill theory, choose **Option B**. There's no wrong choice — log your reasoning in `STUDY-LOG-TEMPLATE.md` so it's part of your record. Either path also works as your one required project for this phase and for your `projects/` portfolio.

## Portfolio output

**One deep learning project** — an image classifier (Option A's course 4, or fast.ai's lesson 1–2 material, both set this up naturally) or a text/NLP model (Option A's course 5, or a fast.ai NLP lesson). It doesn't need to be novel research — a well-executed, well-documented application of an existing technique to a dataset you chose yourself is exactly what this project should be.

## You're done when

- You can explain, at a working level, what a convolutional layer and an attention mechanism each do and why they're structured that way.
- You've trained at least one real neural network on real data, not just a toy example copied verbatim from a lesson.
- Your deep learning project is documented and pushed to `projects/`.
