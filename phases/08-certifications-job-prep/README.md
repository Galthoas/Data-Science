# Phase 8 — Certifications & Job Prep (4–6 weeks, ~70 hrs)

By this phase, the learning is essentially done — this is about converting it into things employers can verify (certificates) and a job search that actually lands interviews.

## Certifications to finalize

Full details on cost, financial aid, and how each is earned are in [`CERTIFICATION-PATHS.md`](../../CERTIFICATION-PATHS.md) at the repo root. Summary:

- **Google Data Analytics Professional Certificate** — earn the credential now if you only audited in Phase 3.
- **Google Advanced Data Analytics Professional Certificate** — same, from Phase 5.
- **IBM Data Science Professional Certificate** (optional but recommended) — https://www.coursera.org/professional-certificates/ibm-data-science — a widely recognized 12-course certificate covering Python, SQL, and applied machine learning; largely overlapping with what you already know by this point, so it moves fast and mainly serves as an additional recognized credential on your resume/LinkedIn.
- **HarvardX Professional Certificate in Data Science** — decide whether the verified-certificate cost (per course) is worth it to you; the skills are already yours either way from Phase 4.

## Resume and LinkedIn

- Rebuild your resume around your four portfolio projects and quantified outcomes, not just course names. "Built and evaluated a classification model achieving X% precision on Y dataset" beats "Completed Google Data Analytics Certificate" as a resume line — list the certificates too, but as credentials supporting the project work, not as the headline.
- Update LinkedIn: headline, About section, and Featured section linking to your GitHub portfolio and best 1–2 projects.
- Consider a short post about your journey (business support technician → data scientist, self-taught via Harvard/MIT/Google coursework) — this kind of story performs well on LinkedIn and is a natural conversation-starter with recruiters.

## Interview preparation

- **SQL:** Practice 20+ problems on a free platform like [StrataScratch](https://www.stratascratch.com/) (has a free tier) or [LeetCode's database problems](https://leetcode.com/problemset/database/) (free).
- **Statistics/ML conceptual questions:** Drill the classic list — p-values, bias/variance, overfitting, regularization, precision vs. recall, A/B testing design. Kaggle and freeCodeCamp forums have free curated question lists; search "data science interview questions" for current, actively maintained lists.
- **Behavioral / "walk me through a project":** Prepare a 3–5 minute walkthrough for each of your four portfolio projects using the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result).
- **Mock interviews:** Practice with a friend, post in relevant free communities (e.g., r/datascience), or record yourself answering out loud — the goal is to stop being surprised by your own explanations.

## Job search mechanics

- Target titles to search, in rough order of how close they are to what this roadmap prepares you for: **Data Analyst → Junior/Associate Data Scientist → Data Scientist**. Given your starting point, a Data Analyst or Junior Data Scientist role is a realistic and smart first move even after completing this entire roadmap — it's a foot in the door that converts to "Data Scientist" within a couple of years of applied work, which is normal and not a step backward.
- Your business support background is an asset, not a gap to hide: frame it explicitly as "I understand how the business actually operates and what decisions data needs to support," which is a genuine differentiator against candidates with only academic backgrounds.
- Keep an applications tracker (a simple spreadsheet is fine) — company, role, date applied, status, follow-up date.

## You're done when

- All target certificates are earned and listed on LinkedIn/resume.
- Your resume and LinkedIn are both rebuilt around your portfolio.
- You've done at least 2 mock interviews and feel calm, not blindsided, by standard SQL/stats/ML questions.
- You're actively applying, with a tracker in place.

This is the end of the structured roadmap — from here it's applying, interviewing, and continuing to build. Update [`progress-tracker.md`](../../progress-tracker.md) one last time and take a minute to look back at how far the repo's commit history has come.
